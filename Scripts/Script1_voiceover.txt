# HACK 1 NIGERIA - Visuals: Sahel village market in harsh sun
In northern Nigeria, heat can spoil vegetables in just a few days. So a teacher named Mohammed Bah Abba popularised an old idea: the pot-in-pot cooler, or zeer.

# HACK 1 - Visuals: hands placing a small pot inside a larger one, pouring sand, then water
Put one clay pot inside a bigger one, fill the gap with wet sand, and cover the top with a damp cloth.

# HACK 1 - Visuals: heat leaving as vapour, then fresh tomatoes inside the pot
As the water evaporates through the outer pot, it pulls heat away, and the inner pot stays much cooler. Tomatoes that would rot in a few days can last for weeks. No electricity, almost no cost.

# HACK 2 BRAZIL / PHILIPPINES - Visuals: a dark tin-roofed room
In 2002, Brazilian mechanic Alfredo Moser had a problem: blackouts and dark homes. His fix was a plastic bottle filled with water and a little bleach, pushed through a hole in the roof.

# HACK 2 - Visuals: bottle sealed into the roof, the room suddenly lights up
Sunlight hits the bottle, the water bends it, and it spreads through the room about as brightly as a 40 to 60 watt bulb.

# HACK 2 - Visuals: aerial view of many rooftops glinting
The Liter of Light movement later spread the idea to hundreds of thousands of homes, starting in the Philippines.

# HACK 3 CHILE - Visuals: fog rolling over dry coastal hills
The Atacama in Chile is one of the driest deserts on Earth, yet thick sea fog called camanchaca rolls in from the Pacific.

# HACK 3 - Visuals: close-up of droplets forming on a net and trickling into a pipe
Villagers stretch huge mesh nets across the hills. Tiny fog droplets stick to the mesh, merge, and drip into gutters below.

# HACK 3 - Visuals: a small green garden in the desert
Some nets collect hundreds of litres a day, enough to grow vegetables and even brew beer.

# HACK 4 LADAKH, INDIA - Visuals: snowy mountain valley
High in the Himalayas, farmers in Ladakh run short of water in spring, just when crops need it most. Engineer Sonam Wangchuk came up with the ice stupa.

# HACK 4 - Visuals: a pipe spraying a fountain that freezes into a tall ice cone
In winter, meltwater is piped downhill and sprayed upward into the freezing air, where it builds a towering cone of ice.

# HACK 4 - Visuals: time-lapse of spring melt flowing into green barley fields
Because a cone exposes less surface to the sun, it melts slowly and releases water right when the fields need it.

# HACK 5 MEGHALAYA, INDIA - Visuals: misty jungle river
And the hack that's alive. In the rainforests of Meghalaya, Khasi villagers guide the roots of rubber fig trees across rivers using hollow betel-nut trunks as guides.

# HACK 5 - Visuals: time-lapse of roots stretching across a gorge
Over 10 to 15 years or more, the roots thicken and weave into a bridge that can carry dozens of people.

# HACK 5 - Visuals: villagers walking over the double-decker root bridge
Unlike wood or steel, it gets stronger as it ages. Some are believed to be centuries old.
