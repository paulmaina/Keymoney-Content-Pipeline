Hack 1: The Clay Fridge – Nigeria (0:35–1:45)
Narration: "In northern Nigeria, heat can spoil vegetables in just a few days. So a teacher named Mohammed Bah Abba popularised an old idea: the pot-in-pot cooler, or zeer. Put one clay pot inside a bigger one, fill the gap with wet sand, and cover the top with a damp cloth. As the water evaporates through the outer pot, it pulls heat away, and the inner pot stays much cooler. Tomatoes that would rot in a few days can last for weeks. No electricity, almost no cost."
Visuals: A Sahel village market in harsh sun. Hands placing a small pot inside a larger one, pouring sand, and then water. An animated arrow shows heat leaving as vapour. Then fresh tomatoes inside.
On-screen text: "Evaporation = free cooling"
Hack 2: The Bottle Light – Brazil and the Philippines (1:45–2:55)
Narration: "In 2002, Brazilian mechanic Alfredo Moser had a problem: blackouts and dark homes. His fix was a plastic bottle filled with water and a little bleach, pushed through a hole in the roof. Sunlight hits the bottle, the water bends it, and it spreads through the room about as brightly as a 40–60 watt bulb. The Liter of Light movement later spread the idea to hundreds of thousands of homes, starting in the Philippines."
Visuals: A dark tin-roofed room. A bottle gets sealed into the roof, and the room suddenly lights up. Then an aerial view of many rooftops glinting.
On-screen text: "1 bottle = 1 free light bulb (daytime)"
Hack 3: Catching Fog – Chile (2:55–4:05)
Narration: "The Atacama in Chile is one of the driest deserts on Earth, yet thick sea fog called camanchaca rolls in from the Pacific. Villagers stretch huge mesh nets across the hills. Tiny fog droplets stick to the mesh, merge, and drip into gutters below. Some nets collect hundreds of litres a day, enough to grow vegetables and even brew beer."
Visuals: Fog rolling over dry coastal hills. Close-up of droplets forming on a net and trickling into a pipe. A small green garden in the desert.
On-screen text: "Water from the air"
Hack 4: Artificial Glaciers – Ladakh, India (4:05–5:15)
Narration: "High in the Himalayas, farmers in Ladakh run short of water in spring, just when crops need it most. Engineer Sonam Wangchuk came up with the ice stupa. In winter, meltwater is piped downhill and sprayed upward into the freezing air, where it builds a towering cone of ice. Because a cone exposes less surface to the sun, it melts slowly and releases water right when the fields need it."
Visuals: Snowy mountain valley. A pipe spraying a fountain that freezes into a tall ice cone. A time-lapse of spring melt flowing into green barley fields.
On-screen text: "A glacier you can build"
Hack 5: Bridges That Grow – Meghalaya, India (5:15–6:20)
Narration: "And the hack that's alive. In the rainforests of Meghalaya, Khasi villagers guide the roots of rubber fig trees across rivers using hollow betel-nut trunks as guides. Over 10 to 15 years or more, the roots thicken and weave into a bridge that can carry dozens of people. Unlike wood or steel, it gets stronger as it ages. Some are believed to be centuries old."
Visuals: Misty jungle river. A time-lapse of roots stretching across a gorge. The final shot shows villagers walking over the double-decker root bridge.
